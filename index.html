CEO Introduction
First in-person Board meeting of 2026 — three days of work inside and outside the boardroom.
For the third consecutive Spring meeting, CEDIA will do important work for the industry inside the Board room as well as in the halls of the U.S. Federal Government.

AI Board Summary — Key Concerns & Watch Points
This is an AI-generated summary for quick orientation.
The full pre-read should be read in its entirety before the meeting.
1
Urgent
Membership decline demands a structural response
Total membership has fallen from 4,205 (2024 YE) to 3,735 (2025 YE) and sits at 2,598 YTD — a trajectory that needs scrutiny even accounting for renewal timing. The company-only renewal rate of 71% sits 10 points below the 81% industry average. Of members who cited reasons for not renewing, 21% said "limited value" — a signal the Board must interrogate directly. Individual membership is down 9% and described as "volatile." The KPI target of 10% growth requires a significant reversal of current trends.
2
Watch Closely
Legislative threats to Technology Integrators are multiplying
CEDIA is actively fighting on five US state fronts simultaneously — Delaware, Georgia, Iowa, Kansas, and two Nebraska bills — all attempting to fold Technology Integrators into electrical contractor licensing frameworks that would impose requirements incompatible with the profession. Nebraska LB 1003 is particularly concerning as it would block TIs from installing renewable energy and battery storage systems entirely. The federal lobby day briefing has been withheld from this pre-read, suggesting there are additional federal-level concerns. The Board should expect a substantial advocacy update in the room.
3
Monitor
CEDIA Expo 2026 carries operational and commercial risk
The team is recovering from a "difficult 2025 partnership year" in which Emerald used CEDIA Expo to test two new platforms — a significant concern for exhibitor and attendee confidence. The 2026 show runs up to a holiday weekend, with "many concerns" acknowledged in the document. Expo is one of CEDIA's most significant revenue events and any attendance or sponsorship shortfall would have a material impact on the full-year financial position. The Board should probe Emerald's commitments and contingency planning.
4
Monitor
Revenue is behind budget — but context matters
Total revenue for January is $215K against a $258K budget, with education and membership dues both short. However, this is one month of data, January is structurally weak for associations, and the operating loss (-$780K) is actually $172K better than the budgeted loss (-$953K). The investment portfolio is the standout positive: $355K returned against a $68K budget. The Board should understand the full-year revenue trajectory and whether Q2/Q3 event revenue is expected to close the gap comfortably.
5
Monitor
ISE membership acquisition collapsed — systemic or one-off?
CEDIA gained just 1 new member through ISE registration in 2026, compared to 21 in 2025. ISE attributes this to a new registration system. While plausible, the Board should seek assurance that this is genuinely a platform issue being resolved for 2027 and not a symptom of declining ISE relevance or brand recognition among prospects. 700 booth scans and 16,000 data points were captured — the conversion of that pipeline into members needs to be tracked closely through Q2.
6
Strategic Opportunity
Design & Build momentum is real — needs sustained investment
The WOWHouse partnership (20,000+ HNW visitors, Chelsea Design Centre, June–July), the NKBA Smart Home Tech award, influencer programme at ISE reaching 1.2M people, and the new D&B Director appointment all represent genuine traction with architects and designers. This is arguably CEDIA's strongest strategic growth vector. The Board should ensure this programme receives protected budget and resource even if broader cost pressures emerge mid-year.
7
Strategic Opportunity
Research department establishment is a defining moment
The hiring of Ryan Bardach as Director of Research and the KPI to deliver credible US and UK market size/scope studies by Expo and EI Live represents CEDIA's first serious attempt to own industry data. If delivered well, this becomes a durable source of authority, media coverage, and member value. The Board should understand the methodology, timeline, and publication strategy — and consider how the findings will be activated commercially and with the press.

Government Affairs
CEDIA continues to monitor and proactively engage across multiple US state legislative sessions.

United States — Active Legislative Issues

Delaware
Board of Electrical Examiners
Board proposed removing low voltage contractor exemption in August. CEDIA testified at multiple meetings. In February, Board confirmed the low voltage exemption will be kept but is now debating whether low voltage work needs to be inspected. A low voltage inspection subcommittee is being considered.
Georgia — SB 553
Electrician Licensing Act Revision
SB 553 repeals and replaces the state's contractor licensing law. Creates four distinct license classes for low voltage contractors with updated definitions and experience requirements. Had committee hearing March 2. CEDIA monitoring amendments.
Iowa — SSB 3148 / SF 2433
National Electrical Code Adoption
As introduced, would have invalidated CEDIA's 15-year-old Technology Integrator exemption from Iowa Electrical Code (Chapter 103). Bill was amended in committee — the amendment adopts the International Energy Conservation Code which does NOT impact CEDIA's exemption. Monitoring for further amendments.
Kansas — HB 2588
Electrician Licensing Act
Creates the Electrician Licensing Act effective July 1, 2027. CEDIA will engage in the rulemaking process before the State Fire Marshal to ensure the scope of work for Technology Integrators is not defined within electrical license requirements.
Nebraska — LB 725
State Electrical Act Expansion
Expands licensing requirements for power limited systems. CEDIA concerns: Class B electrician title does not respect specialised TI training; proposed definition of power limited systems does not align with NEC; no grandfathering for experienced Technology Integrators. CEDIA provided written testimony.
Nebraska — LB 1003
Power Management System Restrictions
Would only allow licensed electrical contractors to install power management systems including renewable energy and battery storage — preventing Technology Integrators from delivering these services. CEDIA provided written testimony and engaged local members.

Outside the US

Australia
Occupational Standards Classification
Submission process for obtaining a formal occupation code opens March 11. CEDIA submitting for formal industry recognition backed by the apprenticeship programme. Census on August 11 will determine outcome. Members being asked to support and use the same classification.
